# Humanizer: natural product copy

## Goal

Make copy sound like a thoughtful product team wrote it for real people. Keep it clear and useful, not artificially casual or aggressively salesy.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, context, desired action, and tone.
2. Preserve the exact meaning and product capability. Do not invent integrations, results, guarantees, metrics, testimonials, or features.
3. Replace vague labels with specific ones. Prefer labels that describe the action or outcome.
4. Remove filler, repetitive phrases, corporate jargon, exaggerated claims, and generic AI-sounding introductions.
5. Use plain language and natural sentence rhythm. Match the product's language and audience; do not force slang or jokes.
6. Keep UI copy concise. A button should usually name an action; helper text should explain what happens or what is needed.
7. For error messages, explain what happened, what the user can do next, and whether their data was saved.
8. For empty states, explain why the area is empty and offer a relevant next action.
9. For landing pages, make the value proposition concrete and support claims with actual capabilities. Avoid false urgency, fake scarcity, and manipulative dark patterns.
10. Preserve domain terms when they are familiar to the audience; explain them when they are not.
11. Review the final copy in context, not as isolated strings. Check consistency across page title, navigation, CTA, helper text, and confirmation.

## Copy patterns

Prefer:
- "Create your first form" over "Initiate form creation"
- "No reports yet" plus a useful next step over "Nothing to see here"
- "We couldn't save your changes. Try again." over "An unexpected error occurred" when the cause is genuinely unknown
- "View deployment logs" over "Explore more" when the destination is logs

These are examples, not mandatory wording. Choose copy appropriate to the actual product.

## Guardrails

- Do not change meaning while editing tone.
- Do not claim the product is secure, compliant, fastest, or best without substantiation.
- Do not add fake social proof or fabricate customer stories.
- Do not hide costs, risks, or limitations behind friendly wording.
- Do not make serious error or security messages cute at the expense of clarity.

## Completion criteria

The copy is clear on first reading, accurate to product behavior, consistent with the interface, and free of unnecessary hype.
