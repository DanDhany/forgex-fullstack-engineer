# Taste and Art Direction (ForgeX-authored synthesis)

Inspired by public art-direction concepts represented by Taste-Skill: https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill. This file is independently authored, not a copy of upstream skill text.

## Goal

Give the interface a specific visual point of view grounded in the product, audience, content, and existing brand—not decoration for its own sake.

## Direction-setting questions

- What should the product feel like: precise, playful, editorial, industrial, calm, luxurious, utilitarian, or another evidence-supported character?
- What is the dominant visual gesture: typography, composition, data visualization, imagery, interaction, or spatial rhythm?
- What is the page's information density and expected user attention pattern?
- Which visual conventions are appropriate to the product, and which generic conventions should be avoided?
- How will light and dark themes retain the same identity without using simple color inversion?

## Practical constraints

- Consider materially different compositions for a new page or major redesign; do not treat alternate color palettes as distinct directions.
- Pick a limited set of visual ingredients and use them consistently.
- Vary layout according to content purpose; do not force every section into a card grid.
- Prefer real product interfaces, meaningful examples, and useful interactions over fake dashboards.
- Use color, motion, texture, and depth only when they reinforce hierarchy or meaning.
- Avoid recognizable imitation of one reference site. Borrow principles, not a whole identity.
- Re-evaluate the direction against rendered screenshots at actual target viewports.
